# Guardrails for sales-intel outreach

These override everything else in the skill if they ever conflict. Read this before finalizing any research claim or outreach message.

The underlying principle: a real salesperson should be comfortable sending every message here after personally researching the account, and every claim in it should survive someone checking the source. If either of those fails, rewrite before showing the output.

## 1. No hallucination

Never invent or assume: revenue, funding, employee count, customers, partnerships, product launches, leadership, technology, locations, expansion, hiring, financial performance, business problems, customer complaints, executive opinions, or individual achievements. Anything not verifiable gets labeled **UNVERIFIED** or **INFERENCE** — never presented as plain fact.

## 2. Source validation

Every important company-specific claim needs a credible source (official site/newsroom > filings/investor materials > official or executive LinkedIn > reputable press > industry publications). Don't lean on one low-quality source for something load-bearing. If two credible sources disagree, flag the conflict and say which looks more reliable rather than picking silently.

## 3. Recency

Prefer signals from the last 6–18 months for "why now" reasoning. An older signal can still be mentioned but must be labeled historical and can't be the primary urgency driver unless it's demonstrably still relevant.

## 4. No fake personalization

Don't write "I saw you're passionate about..." or "I've been following your journey..." or "Congrats on your recent success..." unless there's verified evidence behind it. Never manufacture familiarity the salesperson doesn't actually have.

## 5. No creepy personalization

Even when public, never reference family, relationships, health, religion, politics, ethnicity, sexual orientation, financial difficulty, personal struggles, home/location details, or private social activity. Keep personalization strictly professional.

## 6. No fabricated pain

Never assert "you're struggling with X" unless the company has explicitly stated or clearly demonstrated it. Use hypothesis language instead: "it looks like X may become a challenge as you...", "curious whether X is currently a priority...". Keep FACT visibly separate from HYPOTHESIS.

## 7. No false ROI

Never invent revenue generated, cost savings, conversion lift, ROI, customer counts, efficiency gains, or case-study results for Company A. If Company A has real published proof points, use them — attributed ("Company A reports...") rather than restated as independently verified fact. Otherwise say "can potentially help," not "will increase."

## 8. No guarantee of response

Never claim a message "will definitely work," that a prospect "will respond," or that someone is "the perfect decision-maker." Give a confidence level and the reasoning instead.

## 9. Don't oversell

First touch is about relevance → curiosity → conversation, not closing the sale. No feature dumps, no long product descriptions, no multiple CTAs, no manufactured urgency or scarcity.

## 10. One CTA per message

Every outreach message gets exactly one call to action. Never stack competing asks.

## 11. No generic flattery

Skip "impressed by your company," "love what you're building," "you're a leader in the space" unless it's specific and evidence-backed. Replace with an actual business observation.

## 12. No fake urgency

Don't say "act now" or "limited opportunity" without a real business trigger behind it (expansion, launch, funding, hiring, new market, regulation, strategic initiative).

## 13. Decision-maker accuracy

Never invent a name. If a specific person can't be verified, give a persona instead ("Recommended persona: VP Sales / Head of CX / CFO") and clearly label it as a persona, not a verified contact.

## 14. Contact information

Never generate or guess emails, phone numbers, or direct contact details, and never infer an email address from a naming convention — even if the person is real and verified elsewhere.

## 15. No private data

Don't attempt to access passwords, private accounts/messages, restricted databases, or non-public company information. Use only legitimately public information.

## 16. No deceptive outreach

Never pretend to be someone else, misrepresent the sender, hide commercial intent, invent mutual connections or referrals, or pretend to be an existing customer/user of Company B's product.

## 17. No manipulative psychology

No fear tactics, false scarcity, guilt, shame, threats, fake social proof, or emotional manipulation. Stick to rational business relevance.

## 18. Respect no-response

Don't recommend endless follow-ups. Cap the sequence at 5–6 meaningful touches, and every follow-up must add something new — never send a bare "just checking in" / "following up again" / "did you see my last email."

## 19. Respect opt-outs

If the prospect has said stop/remove me/don't contact me/unsubscribe, the workflow must recommend stopping immediately — never generate another sequence after an explicit opt-out.

## 20. Spam prevention

Don't produce large batches of near-identical messages designed to dodge spam filters, and don't recommend mass identical outreach, keyword stuffing, excessive links/emojis, or fake personalization at scale.

## 21. Company A claims

Classify every Company A claim as VERIFIED / UNVERIFIED / USER-PROVIDED. Only VERIFIED or USER-PROVIDED claims get stated as fact; anything else gets attributed ("Company A reports...").

## 22. Competitor claims

No unsupported negative claims about competitors ("Competitor X is bad/losing customers/doesn't work"). Use neutral differentiation language instead.

## 23. Don't force a fit

Weak product fit, no identifiable need, no credible trigger, no relevant persona, or thin evidence overall → say **"DO NOT OUTREACH YET"** rather than manufacturing a reason. See the main SKILL.md for the exact format.

## 24. Confidence scoring

Every major claim/recommendation carries HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW confidence with a short reason.

## 25. Fact vs. inference labeling

Keep four tiers visibly distinct throughout the research: **FACT** (directly verified), **SIGNAL** (an observable event that may indicate something), **INFERENCE** (a reasonable read on the signal), **HYPOTHESIS** (a potential problem that still needs validation).

## 26. Outreach quality checklist

Before finalizing each message, confirm: specific to this company; personalization is factual; the pain point is supported; "why now" is clear; Company A's relevance is real; it reads human; it's concise; exactly one CTA, low-friction; no generic sales language; no unsupported claims; and it would still make sense if the prospect's name were removed (i.e. it's not just a template with a name pasted in). Any "no" → rewrite.

## 27. Avoid AI-sounding phrasing

Skip: "I hope this message finds you well," "I came across your profile," "I'd love to explore synergies," "I was impressed by...," "given your impressive growth," "leverage," "unlock," "transform your business," "game changer," "revolutionize," "cutting-edge solution." Write like a person who actually did the research.

## 28. Cold-calling ethics

Scripts must identify the caller/company honestly, state the real reason for calling, respect the prospect's time, ask permission where appropriate, and stop immediately if asked not to be contacted.

## 29. Legal/compliance awareness

Never claim a campaign "is legally compliant" — that requires an actual legal/compliance review. Remind the user to check applicable anti-spam, data-protection, telemarketing, and platform rules.

## 30. Final gate — ask before returning output

1. Would a real salesperson be comfortable sending this after personally researching the account? If no, rewrite.
2. Is every important claim supported by evidence or clearly labeled as inference? If no, remove it or label it.
3. Does this give the prospect a legitimate reason to respond? If no, change the angle.

Only return the output once all three pass.
